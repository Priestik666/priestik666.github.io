The operating challenge

Enterprise control without operational paralysis.

Global IT needs common security, architecture, technology and cost controls. Local operations need enough authority to restore service, support production and respond to business reality.

The objective was not decentralization. It was a governed operating model in which central guardrails remain strong while low-risk operational decisions are handled at the level closest to the business.

Operating principles

Standardize what creates leverage. Localize what requires context.

The model separates enterprise-wide controls from decisions that depend on site conditions, local suppliers, production priorities, statutory requirements and immediate operational impact.

Central guardrails

One enterprise foundation.

Common security baseline, architecture principles, approved technologies, lifecycle requirements, auditability and strategic contracts.

Local execution

Authority close to the business.

Low-risk operational actions can be resolved locally within defined controls, with visible ownership and escalation when risk increases.

Shared accountability

Clear decisions, not policy gaps.

Global platform ownership and local business knowledge are connected through explicit roles, service boundaries and decision paths.

The strongest operating model is neither fully centralized nor fully local. It places each decision at the lowest responsible level within common enterprise guardrails.
Complete service lifecycle

Provisioning is only the beginning.

Devices, access, licenses and services create value only when the full lifecycle is governed. Changes, returns, retirement and reuse are operational disciplines, not administrative afterthoughts.

Standard workplace and cost discipline

Lower total cost through repeatability and lifecycle control.

Standard workplace

Reliable, secure and supportable.

Approved device families and common configurations reduce variation and make deployment, support and replacement more predictable.

  • Approved device families
  • Common configuration
  • Modern workplace and M365
  • Consistent security baseline
  • Lower support complexity
License optimization

Right service, right tier, right time.

License cost is managed through enterprise agreements, right-sized subscriptions and timely removal of unused services.

  • Enterprise licensing leverage
  • Role-based license selection
  • Removal during offboarding
  • Reuse before new purchase
  • Visibility of service ownership
Regional execution

Standards adapted to reality.

Enterprise patterns remain intact while local IT works with site needs, production constraints, local vendors and regulatory dependencies.

  • Production and site requirements
  • Local suppliers and lead times
  • Statutory and legacy dependencies
  • Pragmatic operational solutions
  • Escalation when risk exceeds authority
Operational resilience

Design for continuity, not only response.

Resilience combines reliable infrastructure, recoverable services, clear ownership, readiness for change and practical local recovery capability.

  • Service and infrastructure ownership
  • Monitoring and support readiness
  • Documented recovery paths
  • Local impact assessment
  • Continuous improvement
Operational governance

Services need owners, not only tickets.

A ticket can restore a user or service. Governance makes the underlying decision, ownership, priority and history visible so the same problem does not remain trapped in repeated manual work.

Common backlog Operational requests, improvements, risks and dependencies are visible in one managed view.
Defined ownership Each service, action and decision has a responsible owner and an escalation path.
Priority by impact Business impact, operational urgency and risk guide the order of work.
Decision history Important trade-offs and exceptions remain traceable instead of disappearing into chats and email threads.
Enablement by policy Policies define safe boundaries for action rather than becoming a reason for avoidable delay.

Local impact check

Preparation and support

Prepare before go-live.

Align communication, readiness, ownership, fallback options and post-change support.

Business impact

Stable operations with controlled cost and risk.

The model connects workplace standardization, lifecycle governance, local authority, cost discipline and operational readiness into one practical operating system.

Regional operations become a controlled environment for validating improvements before wider adoption, without weakening enterprise accountability.

Faster resolution of low-risk operational issues Defined local authority reduces avoidable queues while preserving escalation for higher-risk decisions.
Lower workplace complexity Common devices, configurations and services make support and lifecycle planning more predictable.
Better license and asset economics Right-sizing, removal, return and reuse reduce unnecessary consumption.
Stronger ownership and transparency Services, operational work and decisions remain visible beyond individual incidents.
More reliable change adoption Local impact checking and preparation connect enterprise change to business continuity.
Scalable regional learning Validated operational patterns can be refined and reused across a wider organization.
Leadership insight

We are not asking for exceptions. We are creating a controlled environment for validation.

Strong governance does not require every operational action to travel through the same central queue. It requires clear standards, explicit decision rights, accountable ownership and reliable evidence that the model works.
